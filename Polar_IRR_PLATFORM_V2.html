SIG Annotation Guidelines

Important Rules Before Marking ▼
  • Session start: Polar often needs 1–2 minutes to stabilize. Always check the beginning of each session.
  • Minimum duration: Annotated segments should be 5–10+ seconds. Ignore brief blips.
  • Merge nearby regions: If the gap between two bad segments is under ~30 seconds, merge into one trim.
  • Impossible values: Flag Polar below 30 BPM or above 220 BPM.
  • Data gaps: Missing Polar data or values of 0 should NOT be marked. Only flag when Polar is present but wrong.
  • WHOOP-only errors: Do not mark Polar merely because WHOOP looks wrong. Investigate: check other sensors, activity context, signal shape, and motion.
  • Be conservative: When evidence is balanced, leave the region unmarked rather than guessing.

Patterns to Flag

1. Flatline / Lockout

HR stays constant (e.g. 80 BPM) for an extended period while WHOOP shows normal variation. Often happens at session start when the strap hasn't locked onto the heartbeat yet.

2. Jump(s)

Abrupt HR changes — sudden jumps up or down that don't match WHOOP. Can be a single spike or multiple sequential jumps. Often appears as a "staircase" pattern.

3. Noisy Polar / Spikes

High-frequency noise, erratic spikes, or jittery signal. The trace looks "fuzzy" compared to WHOOP's smooth line. Individual spikes to extreme values (>220 or <30 BPM) also fall here.

4. Large Mismatch with WHOOP

Polar and WHOOP differ by 20+ BPM for at least 30 seconds. Also mark slow drift where Polar settles at a clearly wrong level for several minutes after a jump. Polar may look smooth here — this is the only pattern that cannot be judged from the Polar line alone.

Show criteria \u25BC

Multi-sensor logic:

  • Sensors within ~10 BPM of each other and all disagreeing with Polar = strong evidence, mark Polar.
  • Sensors spread >10 BPM apart = no reliable reference, leave it.
  • At least half the sensors disagreeing with Polar by 20+ BPM = mark Polar.

Tie-breakers (in order):

  1. Activity context — 150 BPM at rest or 55 BPM mid-sprint is unlikely regardless of source.
  2. Shape — real HR ramps and recovers smoothly; which line behaves physiologically?
  3. Motion — during high motion, trust WHOOP less; during rest/sleep, trust it more.

Do NOT mark when WHOOP sensors disagree among themselves, when only WHOOP looks wrong and Polar remains smooth and plausible, or for differences shorter than 30 seconds. If evidence is balanced, leave unmarked.

5. Impossible Value

HR reading below 30 BPM or above 220 BPM, which are physiologically impossible in the study context.

Decision Tree

1Is Polar present? (not missing, not 0) — No → data gap, leave it.
2Polar <30 or >220 BPM? — Yes → impossible value, mark it.
3Flatline? Same value (±1 BPM) for 30+ s while WHOOP fluctuates — mark (unless context explains low steady HR at rest/sleep).
4Jump? 25+ BPM step in <5 s — check samples are continuous (not a gap). If real step and WHOOP disagrees → mark.
5Noisy / spiky? 15+ BPM spike & back in 5 s, or 25+ BPM swings over 1–2 min while WHOOP is stable → mark.
6Large mismatch? Polar vs WHOOP 20+ BPM apart for 30+ s → check corroboration (see mismatch card below).
7Smooth and believable? → Leave it.
